LESSON OVERVIEW
===============
So far your robot can only move in straight lines. Time to change that!
In this lesson, you'll learn how to turn your robot left and right.
By combining turns with forward movement, your robot can navigate
anywhere - just like a real car!

Time Required: 60-75 minutes
Difficulty: Intermediate
Prerequisites: Lessons 01-03


LESSON OBJECTIVES
=================
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  1. Turn the robot left and right
  2. Understand how the motors create a turn
  3. Combine turning with forward movement
  4. Estimate how long to turn for specific angles (90 degrees, etc.)
  5. Create a square driving pattern


VOCABULARY
==========
  Rotation    - Spinning in place. The robot turns without moving
                forward or backward.

  Clockwise   - Turning to the RIGHT, like clock hands move.
                (Think: the top of the robot moves to the right)

  Counter-    - Turning to the LEFT, opposite of clock hands.
  clockwise     (Think: the top of the robot moves to the left)

  Degrees     - A way to measure how much you turn.
                90 degrees = a quarter turn (right angle)
                180 degrees = half turn (facing opposite direction)
                360 degrees = full turn (back where you started)

  Right angle - A 90-degree turn. The corners of a square are all
                right angles. This is the most common turn you'll use.


NEW COMMANDS YOU WILL LEARN
===========================

  turnLeft(speed, duration)
  -------------------------
  What it does:  Makes the robot spin to the LEFT (counterclockwise).

  How to use it: car.turnLeft(speed, duration);

  Parameters:
    speed    - How fast to turn (0-255). Higher = faster turning.
    duration - How long to turn, in milliseconds.

  Example:     car.turnLeft(150, 500);
               Turns left at medium speed for 0.5 seconds.
               This is approximately a 90-degree turn on most robots.

  How it works:
    To turn LEFT, the robot's right motor spins FORWARD and the left
    motor spins BACKWARD. This makes the robot spin in place, like
    a spinning top.

    Right wheel goes forward    -->  Robot turns left
    Left wheel goes backward    <--

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------

  turnRight(speed, duration)
  --------------------------
  What it does:  Makes the robot spin to the RIGHT (clockwise).

  How to use it: car.turnRight(speed, duration);

  Parameters:
    speed    - How fast to turn (0-255).
    duration - How long to turn, in milliseconds.

  Example:     car.turnRight(150, 500);
               Turns right at medium speed for 0.5 seconds.

  How it works:
    To turn RIGHT, the left motor spins FORWARD and the right motor
    spins BACKWARD. Opposite of a left turn!

    Left wheel goes forward     -->  Robot turns right
    Right wheel goes backward   <--


UNDERSTANDING ANGLES AND DURATION
==================================
The longer you turn, the more degrees the robot rotates:

  Duration (approximate, at speed 150):
    250 ms ≈  45 degrees (1/8 of a full turn)
    500 ms ≈  90 degrees (1/4 of a full turn - right angle)
    750 ms ≈ 135 degrees (3/8 of a full turn)
   1000 ms ≈ 180 degrees (1/2 of a full turn - U-turn)
   2000 ms ≈ 360 degrees (full turn - back to start)

  IMPORTANT: These are ESTIMATES! Every robot is slightly different.
  You MUST test your robot to find the exact values.

  How to calibrate your turns:
    1. Place robot facing a wall or tape line on the floor
    2. Run turnRight(150, 500);
    3. Measure how far it turned
    4. If it turned less than 90 degrees, increase the time
    5. If it turned more than 90 degrees, decrease the time
    6. Write down your 90-degree value - you'll use it a lot!


COMBINING TURNS WITH FORWARD MOVEMENT
======================================
A turn by itself just spins the robot in place. To actually go
somewhere new, you combine turns with forward movement:

  // Go forward, turn right, go forward (makes an "L" shape)
  car.moveForward(180, 1000);  // Go straight
  delay(300);                   // Brief pause
  car.turnRight(150, 500);     // Turn 90 degrees right
  delay(300);                   // Brief pause
  car.moveForward(180, 1000);  // Go straight in new direction

  This is how your robot navigates! Every path is just a series of:
    1. Go forward some distance
    2. Turn some angle
    3. Go forward some more
    4. Repeat!


COMPLETE CODE WITH EXPLANATIONS
================================

  #include <CarControl.h>
  CarControl car(5, 6, 7, 8, 3, 2);

  void setup() {
    Serial.begin(9600);
    Serial.println("Lesson 04: Turning Left and Right");
    car.setup();
    Serial.println("TIP: Watch how long it takes to turn 90 degrees!");
  }

  void loop() {
    // Example 1: Simple left turn
    Serial.println("Turning left");
    car.turnLeft(150, 500);      // Turn left for 0.5 seconds
    delay(2000);

    // Example 2: Simple right turn
    Serial.println("Turning right");
    car.turnRight(150, 500);     // Turn right for 0.5 seconds
    delay(2000);

    // Example 3: Move forward then turn (L-shape)
    Serial.println("Move and turn (making an L)");
    car.moveForward(180, 1000);  // Forward
    delay(500);
    car.turnRight(150, 500);     // Turn right 90 degrees
    delay(500);
    car.moveForward(180, 1000);  // Forward in new direction
    delay(2000);

    // Example 4: First corner of a square
    Serial.println("First corner of a square");
    car.moveForward(180, 1000);  // Side 1
    delay(300);
    car.turnRight(150, 500);     // Corner
    delay(300);
    car.moveForward(180, 1000);  // Side 2
    delay(2000);

    // Example 5: Spinning!
    Serial.println("Spinning right for 2 seconds!");
    car.turnRight(180, 2000);    // Full spin right
    delay(1000);
    Serial.println("Spinning left for 2 seconds!");
    car.turnLeft(180, 2000);     // Full spin left
    delay(2000);

    car.stopMotors();
    delay(5000);
  }


STUDENT CHALLENGE
=================

  MAIN CHALLENGE: Make a Square!

  Program your robot to drive in a PERFECT SQUARE pattern.

  A square has:
    - 4 equal sides
    - 4 right-angle turns (90 degrees each)

  The algorithm (step-by-step plan):
    Step 1: Move forward (Side 1)
    Step 2: Turn right 90 degrees
    Step 3: Move forward (Side 2)
    Step 4: Turn right 90 degrees
    Step 5: Move forward (Side 3)
    Step 6: Turn right 90 degrees
    Step 7: Move forward (Side 4)
    Step 8: Turn right 90 degrees (back to start!)

  Code Template:
    void loop() {
      Serial.println("Drawing a square!");

      // Side 1
      Serial.println("Side 1");
      car.moveForward(180, 1000);
      delay(300);
      car.turnRight(150, _____);    // Find your 90-degree time!
      delay(300);

      // Side 2
      Serial.println("Side 2");
      car.moveForward(180, 1000);
      delay(300);
      car.turnRight(150, _____);
      delay(300);

      // Side 3
      Serial.println("Side 3");
      car.moveForward(180, 1000);
      delay(300);
      car.turnRight(150, _____);
      delay(300);

      // Side 4
      Serial.println("Side 4");
      car.moveForward(180, 1000);
      delay(300);
      car.turnRight(150, _____);
      delay(300);

      Serial.println("Square complete!");
      delay(5000);
    }

  Steps to complete:
    1. First, find your 90-degree turn time
       - Try car.turnRight(150, 500); and see if it's 90 degrees
       - Adjust up or down until you get it right
    2. Fill in the blank _____ with your 90-degree time
    3. Test the full square
    4. Adjust the forward time so all sides look equal

  Bonus Challenges:
    1. Make a TRIANGLE (3 sides, 120-degree turns)
    2. Make a RECTANGLE (2 long sides, 2 short sides)
    3. Make your robot drive the square TWICE in a row
    4. Make the square using LEFT turns instead of right turns


MATH CONNECTION
===============
  For any regular shape (all sides equal, all angles equal):

  Turn angle = 360 ÷ number of sides

  Triangle:  360 ÷ 3 = 120 degrees per turn
  Square:    360 ÷ 4 = 90 degrees per turn
  Pentagon:  360 ÷ 5 = 72 degrees per turn
  Hexagon:   360 ÷ 6 = 60 degrees per turn

  So if you know your 90-degree turn time, you can calculate others:
    If 90 degrees = 500 ms:
    120 degrees = 500 × (120/90) = 667 ms
    60 degrees  = 500 × (60/90)  = 333 ms


COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID
=========================
  1. Not adding delays between movements and turns
  2. Using different forward times for each side of the square
     (all sides should be the same!)
  3. Not calibrating the 90-degree turn first
  4. Confusing left and right (it happens to everyone!)
  5. Making the robot go too fast for accurate turns


TEACHER NOTES
=============
  - Have students find their 90-degree turn time FIRST before
    attempting the square challenge
  - Create a class calibration chart for different speeds
  - Use tape on the floor to mark the square path
  - Discuss: What happens if the battery is low? (turns get shorter)
  - Great lesson for introducing geometry concepts
  - Pair students: one holds a stopwatch, other watches angles
  - Extension: Have students calculate the perimeter of their square
